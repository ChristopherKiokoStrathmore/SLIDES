How prepared is Kenya for a disease outbreak?

On 13 March 2020 the question stopped being hypothetical.

Kenya confirmed its first COVID-19 case, a traveller from London. Within days every county needed an answer to one question: if this spreads, do we have the beds? Nobody could say. Yet the answer had been sitting in a public spreadsheet since 2017.

The task we set

Turn a register of buildings into a falsifiable claim about people, one that can be proved wrong, county by county, before the next outbreak rather than during it.

H: Kenya's existing health infrastructure has sufficient inpatient capacity to absorb a moderate respiratory outbreak without counties exceeding their surge capacity.

The thresholds were fixed before the data was touched, so the result could not be tuned after the fact.

Every facility in Kenya, one point each

8,932 facilities. An impressive number, and the reason national summaries sound reassuring. Now filter it.

AAction, what we did

So we asked all 47 counties the same question.

Not "how many beds does Kenya have", but "how far can this spread before you run out?" Then we let the wave move, and watched who fell first.

How we got there

1
We counted every bed. Inpatient beds and cots in all 8,932 facilities: 60,814 places for the whole country.
2
We counted the people. The 2019 census, county by county: 47.56 million Kenyans each county has to cover.
3
Then we asked when one runs out of the other. For every county, the share of its people who can fall ill before its beds are gone.

Kenya has 12.8 beds per 10,000 people. The WHO put it at 13.3 for 2019, so the register is telling the truth about beds.
